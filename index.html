How taxes enter netback

Aligned with RK Tax Code practice: world price for export rental & MET is the relevant grade (here Kebco). Netback subtracts those $/t charges plus transport.

1 · Fiscal marker (model)
Kebco = Brent − Skebco
Proxy for Argus “Kebco – CIF Augusta”. Skebco may be negative. Official MET/rental world price may be Kebco, Urals or Brent Dtd by contract (Tax Code art. 776).
2 · Export rental tax (art. 749)
Rental = rate(Kebco) × Kebco × rental bbl factor
Progressive % of world price by $/bbl band (0–32%). Ladder thresholds match the Code’s “up to $X/bbl” schedule.
3 · Mineral extraction tax — export (arts. 776–778)
Export MET = Kebco × MET% × MET bbl factor
Export / marketable oil: tax base = volume × world price (Urals / Kebco / Brent Dtd by contract). 2026 rates 5–18% by annual production (model default 5%).
4 · Domestic fiscal (art. 778 + VAT)
Ex-VAT local = local KZT ÷ FX ÷ VAT factor
Domestic MET = ex-VAT local × MET% × domestic factor
Rental = 0 · Export duty = 0 (export-only)
2026 Code: domestic sales/transfers to RK refineries get a 0.5 reduction on the MET rate (art. 778). Tax base for refinery sales is the actual purchase price (art. 776) — model uses ex-VAT local $/t as that proxy. VAT standard rate 16% (factor 1.16) is stripped from the posted local price; it is not an extra netback deduction. From 2027 the 0.5 factor is replaced by a published equalizing coefficient.
5 · Export customs duty
Duty = duty ladder(Kebco) $/t · domestic = n/a
Official MoF rate uses prior-period average of Kebco and Brent (not Kebco alone); $25–105 → duty ≈ that average $/t; above $105 → fixed schedule. This model looks up the schedule on Kebco as a single-marker proxy.
6 · Netbacks
Export NB = (Brent − Skebco − Discount) × bbl/t − transport − duty − rental − export MET
Domestic NB = ex-VAT local − domestic transport − domestic MET
Δ = Export NB − Domestic NB
Discount is commercial (realized price), separate from fiscal Kebco. Transport = Σ(KZT + RUB×RUB→KZT) ÷ FX.
